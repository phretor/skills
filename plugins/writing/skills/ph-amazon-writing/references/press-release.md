# Press Release (PR) Format

A press release is the opening narrative of a PR/FAQ document. It announces a product or feature from the customer's perspective, written as if the product already exists. The PR is always under one page.

## Structure

### Headline

`[Product Name] — [One-line customer benefit]`

The headline states what the product is and why it matters to the customer in a single line.

### Opening Paragraph

`[City, Date]` — One paragraph announcing what is being launched and why it matters to customers. Lead with the customer benefit, not the technology.

### Leadership Quote

A quote from a leader framing the announcement in customer-focused terms. The quote should convey conviction about the customer benefit, not describe internal engineering effort.

### The Customer Problem

Two to three sentences describing the specific customer pain point or opportunity. Be concrete about who the customer is, when and where the problem occurs, and what frustrates them. Use data where available.

### How [Product Name] Solves This

Two to three paragraphs describing the end-state customer experience. Write as if the product exists today. Focus on what customers can now do, not on how the product was built. Use concrete scenarios.

### Customer Quote

A quote from a beta customer describing a specific benefit they experienced. The quote should be believable and reference a measurable improvement.

### Availability

When and how customers can access the product.

## Writing Rules for Press Releases

1. **Write from the customer's perspective.** The reader is a customer, not an internal stakeholder. Every sentence should answer "why should the customer care?"
2. **Be specific.** Replace "many customers face challenges" with "73% of IT managers spend over 4 hours per week on manual compliance checks."
3. **No internal jargon.** The press release is a public-facing document. Terms like "flywheel," "two-pizza team," or internal project names do not belong.
4. **State the benefit before the feature.** "Customers can now deploy in under 60 seconds" comes before "powered by our new orchestration engine."
5. **Keep it under one page.** The constraint forces clarity. If you cannot explain the customer value in one page, the value proposition is not sharp enough.
6. **The product already exists.** Write in present tense. "Customers can" not "customers will be able to."

## Common Mistakes

- Leading with the technology instead of the customer problem
- Using vague qualifiers ("significantly better," "industry-leading") instead of data
- Writing a marketing brochure instead of a news announcement
- Including internal metrics (cost savings, team velocity) that don't matter to the customer
- Making the leadership quote generic rather than specific to the customer benefit
