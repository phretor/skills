# Speaker Archetypes

Mapped on a quadrant of **technical depth** (low → high) × **communication style** (informational → motivational). Use as a *lens* for tuning coaching tone — not as a box.

| Archetype  | Quadrant                       | Strength                                       | Most common pitfall                      |
| ---------- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Analyst    | High technical / informational | Precision, data, reproducibility               | "Death by detail"; never reaches "so what" |
| Visionary  | High technical / motivational  | Mobilizes across teams; "why now / what's next" | Buzzword-heavy, vague on implementation  |
| Educator   | Mid/low technical / informational | Translates jargon; builds workforce capability | Slideshow-style monotony; oversimplifies |
| Inspirer   | Low technical / motivational   | Emotional resonance, outreach, identity        | Cliché territory; missing call-to-action |

## Self-assessment (use in design mode prelude)

Ask the speaker:

1. **Who do you feel most confident speaking to?** Technical peers / leadership / students / general → primary archetype.
2. **What feedback do you get most often?**
   - "Insightful and detailed" → Analyst
   - "Made me think differently" → Visionary
   - "You explained that so clearly" → Educator
   - "That story moved me" → Inspirer
3. **What frustrates you in talks?**
   - Too vague → Analyst
   - Too tactical → Visionary
   - Too jargony → Educator
   - Too dry → Inspirer
4. **What goal drives you when you prepare?**
   - Share data → Analyst
   - Inspire vision → Visionary
   - Teach something → Educator
   - Connect emotionally → Inspirer

The dominant answer is the archetype. The next-highest answer is the *growth edge* the coach should push toward without forcing.

## Per-archetype exercises (offer during design mode if speaker is stuck)

- **Analyst — "1-1-3 summary"**: condense a whitepaper into 1 sentence (core message), 1 paragraph (context + result), 3 bullets (what to remember).
- **Visionary — "What's Next? pitch"**: status quo → urgent challenge → bold vision → concrete first step.
- **Educator — "Two-minute teach"**: pick a topic (zero trust, DNSSEC, JWT) and explain it to a high schooler with no jargon. Record; repeat clearer.
- **Inspirer — "Story with a point"**: 2–3 minute story about a moment that changed your perspective in cybersecurity, ending with what the audience should take away (not what *you* learned).

## Coaching rule

The most effective speakers blend styles. Never tell a speaker their archetype is "wrong." Identify the dominant style, then push toward the adjacent quadrant for range.
